A stranger rides into town, he notices several things as he awakes from his nap. The trees of this town are incredibly tall. He smells the wonderful aromas of teas and spices coming from the small shop on his right as he passes by. He thinks to himself about the tea he would drink with his grandfather on the cold mornings and listen to the old man’s wild stories. The stranger sees kids and farmers in the street, several of whom pay him no mind and too busy to have any concerns for another traveler but a few still manage to have a curious glance here and there. He starts to hear music as he arrives closer to the center of town. One musician after another, he hears different genres and ballads almost luring him into the lively Inn. He passes by the temple to Pelor, this one not as big as the ones he has seen closer to the capital but just as ornate and beautiful as the other temples he has seen. Finally he crosses the middle of town where a gathering of merchants and wagons have clustered, peddling their wares and selling necessary goods. The haggling of the townsfolk and merchants carries on as the stranger notices an odd sight. In the distance there is a hooded figure looking at him, almost glaring. As the stranger approaches them the figure rushes into the crowd and disappears. The once comfortable nature of this town has taken a turn for the unease for the stranger.
“Hopefully,” he thought, “I won’t have to stay here for too long.”

Tired, hungry, and frustrated, Sheriff Stownward walks into town from a nearby domestic dispute. Still thinking of how much of a waste of time arguing over who harvested who’s crops was, she was ready to grab her favorite Wild Mushroom Soup from the Inn. She noticed that the town was busier than usual. She greets each of the villagers and their children as they run by her and as she walks by them. The Emerald Wyrm has more patrons today and she can see a few familiar and unfamiliar faces. Someone is on the makeshift stage in the corner reading from a notebook out loud, probably another poet. She makes her way further down the main road and finally at long last can get her soup! She walks into the inn with its loud and obnoxious music. She thought she would enjoy the daily tunes but they have had more bad songs than good ones lately. After receiving her meal, Sheriff Stoneward takes her order to go and continues to the central market where just before she checks in on Thomas Blythe in the [[General Store]] to make sure the kind shopkeeper is doing okay with their new apprentice. 
The now nourished sheriff makes her way to the market seeing that the Temple to Pelor is starting to light their large incense and prayer hearths. She stops by the temple and sneaks a peek at the [[Seed of Drikolia]] to ensure it is still in its altar, nestled away behind the fancy podium. Sufficiently comforted that everything is right and correct, she moves to the center of town and the gathering stage. There she sees why the town is lively today. The merchant caravan has finally arrived and the center is full of trinkets and textiles, exotic foods and wines, but most importantly, commerce for the town. Satisfied with the day’s activities the sleepy sheriff heads back to her post to rest up for tonight’s watch.

It wasn’t a long ride to the town of Blythewood but it was a boring one, thought the king, as he arrived by carriage to the busy town. King Grayson always rode by carriage, he was not permitted to do otherwise outside the castle walls. He was still having a hard time accepting his new title, he wanted so dearly to have his mother and father back but this was the reality for a royal orphan. Hearing so much about the bards and music and poetry available in this backwoods settlement definitely piqued his interest when being given the list of options of things to do other than being allowed to sulk in the capital. Grayson hears the guards yell and tell the townsfolk to step aside from the carriage. He peers out the window and sees everyone staring at the procession. He thinks to himself maybe this was a bad idea, not wanting this kind of attention. The wagon comes to a halt and a guard opens his door. Grayson is met with a beautiful town. With trees much more grand than he could have hoped. Ahead of him, he can sense the music and entertainment unfolding inside and eagerly makes his way indoors.
When Grayson enters the Inn he is me with a sudden hush and many staring eyes. Carefully, he gestures for a space in the corner and several guards disperse and he tries to remain inconspicuous as he takes his seat. Then, slowly but surely, the music begins again and the crowd appears to have all but forgotten him. “Here”, Grayson thought, “I may be able to be a little normal.”
